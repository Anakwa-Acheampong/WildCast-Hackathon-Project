Know before you go.

Tourists travel twelve hours to Mole hoping to see an elephant — and whether they do is pure luck. WildCast changes that. Our AI studies what park guides see every day, and turns it into a simple daily forecast: which animals, which part of the park, what time of day. Like a weather forecast — but for wildlife.

Never heard of a wildlife forecast? It's this simple.

01

Guides record what they see

Every day, park guides already spot elephants, antelope and baboons on their walks. They tap it into a simple phone form — ten seconds per sighting.

02

The AI learns the patterns

Animals are creatures of habit — waterholes at dawn in the dry season, shade at midday. Our model learns these rhythms from thousands of sightings, weather and seasons.

03

You get a forecast

Before and during your visit, you see the odds: "Elephants — 82% this morning at the waterholes." Plan your walk around it. Leave with memories, not maybes.

How it works

Simple pipeline. Honest AI.

No black boxes — here's exactly what the system does, what data feeds it, and what it automates.

01.

Collect

Guides and rangers log sightings on a simple offline-first form. Historical patrol records seed the system from day one — no cold start.

Guide sighting logs SMART ranger patrol data GMet weather
02.

Learn

A gradient-boosted probability model learns each species' rhythms — season, time of day, rainfall, waterhole levels — and outputs a likelihood per zone, refreshed daily.

Season & rainfall Time of day Zone history
03.

Forecast

Visitors see plain-language odds on the web — before booking and during the visit. Park managers get the same data as visitor-flow and conservation insight.

Daily forecast map Seasonal planner Wildlife passport
🛡️

Designed so it can never help a poacher.

Locations are shown at zone level only — never GPS points. Public forecasts run on a time delay, and sensitive species (like pangolin) are excluded from all public views. Precise data stays inside the Forestry Commission's existing SMART patrol system.

Built on SMART — the Spatial Monitoring and Reporting Tool Ghana's rangers already use. From protected areas to SMART destinations, literally.
